# Derivatives of the founder letter

All of these are cut down from `our-story.md`. Rule: the long version lives in
exactly one place on the site. Everything else is a short version that links to
it. Never publish two full-length tellings — that reads as a marketing asset
instead of a person.

---

## 1. Homepage "why" block (110 words)

> **Why we exist**
>
> My father died in January 2021. My mother died five months later. I'm an only
> child, so their houses came to me — along with a newborn, a job, and no idea
> what any of it was worth.
>
> I had sold over a million dollars of other people's things online. I still
> couldn't do it. I rented a storage unit and paid to avoid it for years.
>
> VaultXL is the thing I needed then. You get an inventory, real numbers, and a
> plan before anything is sold — so you don't have to become an expert in the
> worst month of your life.
>
> — Charles Dewhurst, Founder · [Read the whole story →]

## 3. LinkedIn post (the single highest-value use of this story)

This is where estate attorneys, CPAs, realtors, and senior-move managers are —
the people who send referrals. Post it once, from Charles's personal profile,
not the company page. Do not boost it.

> I rented a storage unit for [N] years so I wouldn't have to look inside it.
>
> My dad died on January 6, 2021. My mom died five months later. I'm an only
> child. Both houses came to me, along with a four-month-old and a full-time job.
>
> The part that still gets me: I had sold over a million dollars of other
> people's things online at that point. I can price a pair of Jordans within five
> dollars from a photo. And I could not tell you what my mother's living room was
> worth, or what to sell first, or what to keep for my daughter.
>
> Expertise wasn't what I was missing. Capacity was. Grief takes the exact part
> of your brain that settling an estate requires.
>
> So I put it all in storage and paid [$XXX] a month to postpone the decision.
> That's where most estates go. The value drops while the bill runs.
>
> I built VaultXL because of that unit. We inventory an estate, price it against
> what each category actually sells for, and route each piece to the channel that
> pays the most — before anything moves. [N] families so far.
>
> If you're an attorney or a realtor who watches families hit this wall, I'd like
> to talk.

## 5. First-client letter (send after the walkthrough, before the contract)

Not public. This is the piece that actually closes business in this category,
because the objection is never price — it's trust with a dead parent's things.

> [Name],
>
> Thank you for walking me through the house on [date].
>
> I want you to know why I do this. I settled my own parents' estates in 2021 —
> my father in January, my mother five months later. I'm an only child. I did it
> badly, and I did it alone, and a lot of it ended up in a storage unit I'm still
> paying for.
>
> So: nothing in your parents' house gets sold without your written sign-off, and
> anything you flag as staying with the family stays, no questions and no
> pressure, even if it's the most valuable thing in the room.
>
> Attached is the inventory and what I think each category brings. Numbers I'm
> confident in are marked; ranges mean I want a second opinion before we commit.
>
> Take your time with it.
>
> Charles
