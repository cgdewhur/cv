# Derivatives of the founder letter

All of these are cut down from `our-story.md`. Rule: the long version lives in
exactly one place — `/why-vaultxl`. Everything else is short and links to it.
Never publish two full-length tellings; that reads as a marketing asset instead
of a person.

---

## 1. Homepage "why" block (115 words)

> **Why we exist**
>
> VaultXL didn't start with a business plan. It started with a storage unit.
>
> My father died in January 2021. My mother died five months later. I'm an only
> child, so both households came to me — along with a newborn, a job, and no idea
> what any of it was worth.
>
> I had sold over a million dollars of other people's things online. I still
> couldn't do it. I rented a unit and paid every month to postpone the decision.
>
> That's not a death problem. It's a transition problem, and it shows up in
> downsizing, moving, divorce and retirement too. VaultXL is the system I needed
> and didn't have.
>
> — Charles Dewhurst, Founder · [Read the whole story →]

## 2. About page (third person, ~260 words — press kit, partnerships, decks)

> ## Built from personal experience
>
> VaultXL began with a problem its founder couldn't solve for himself.
>
> Charles Dewhurst spent more than a decade in e-commerce before starting the
> company. He opened his first eBay account with his father in 1999, turned a
> sneaker habit into a footwear business, and went on to run e-commerce for other
> organizations — including the Carolina Panthers' official team store and a
> German manufacturer's U.S. operation. Before that he played Division I
> basketball at UNC Charlotte and professionally overseas.
>
> In January 2021 his father died. His mother died five months later. As an only
> child with a four-month-old at home, Dewhurst inherited two households and the
> question that comes with them: what do you do with all of this?
>
> Some of it had monetary value. Some had only sentimental value. Most of it
> needed research, photography, pricing, and a decision about where it should be
> sold — or whether it should be sold at all.
>
> Even with a decade of e-commerce behind him, doing that while grieving,
> working, and raising an infant proved impossible. So, like millions of
> families, he put everything into storage. It stayed there for years.
>
> That experience became VaultXL: technology, logistics and e-commerce built for
> the moments when belongings outnumber the capacity to deal with them. Estates
> and downsizing, but also relocation, assisted living, divorce, retirement,
> inheritance and post-sale liquidation.
>
> VaultXL is based in Charlotte, North Carolina, and serves [service area].
>
> **Help people move forward without leaving everything behind.**

## 5. Founder video (2–4 minutes)

Shoot it in the storage unit. Not a studio, not an office — the actual unit,
door open, you standing in it. That single visual does more than any line of
copy, and it's the shot every local news producer will want.

**Structure — you speaking, cut over stills:**

| Time | Voiceover beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | "VaultXL didn't start with a business plan. It started with a storage unit." | You, inside the unit, door open behind you |
| 0:15 | Basketball → the end of it → no résumé | Old game photos, UNCC, overseas |
| 0:40 | eBay with dad in 1999, sneakers, the business | Early eBay screenshots, sneaker shots, shipping table |
| 1:10 | 2020: baby coming, world coming apart, parents aging | Family photo, your daughter as a newborn |
| 1:30 | The call from Vermont. The empty plane. The hospital door. | Snow, the house, then hold on black for the date |
| 2:10 | Mom, five months later | Photo of her |
| 2:30 | "I'd sold a million dollars of other people's things. I couldn't price my own mother's living room." | Her house / her things |
| 2:50 | "Expertise wasn't what I was missing. Capacity was." | You, to camera, no cutaway |
| 3:05 | The unit. Three years. The bill. | The unit again, wider |
| 3:20 | What VaultXL does now — and that it's not only about death | Work footage: cataloging, photographing, a client handoff |
| 3:45 | "Help people move forward without leaving everything behind." | You, then logo |

Rules: no music under the hospital section. No B-roll of stock families. If you
get emotional, keep the take.

## 7. First-client letter (private; send after walkthrough, before contract)

The objection in this business is never price. It's trust with a dead parent's
things.

> [Name],
>
> Thank you for walking me through the house on [date].
>
> I want you to know why I do this. I settled my own parents' estates in 2021 —
> my father in January, my mother five months later. I'm an only child. I did it
> badly and I did it alone, and a lot of it ended up in a storage unit I'm still
> paying for.
>
> So: nothing gets sold without your written sign-off, and anything you flag as
> staying with the family stays — no questions, no pressure — even if it's the
> most valuable thing in the room.
>
> Attached is the inventory and what I think each category brings. Numbers I'm
> confident in are marked; ranges mean I want a second opinion before we commit.
>
> Take your time with it.
>
> Charles
