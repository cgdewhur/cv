# Press release — hold until there is news

A press release announces an **event**. "I have a moving founding story" is not
an event, and a release built on it reads as grief used as a marketing device —
the exact impression VaultXL cannot afford with this audience.

Send this only when one of these is true:

- VaultXL formally launches (LLC + site + service area live)
- A funding round, acquisition, or named partnership closes
- A real milestone lands: Nth estate settled, $X returned to families, a new market
- A partnership with a law firm, senior-living community, or estate-attorney network

Until then, use the media pitch in `derivatives.md`. Reporters run pitches. They
do not run press releases.

When the day comes, the story goes in **one paragraph and one quote** — no more.
The release is about the news; the story is the reason the reporter keeps reading.

## VaultXL launches in Charlotte to handle the belongings families leave behind in estates, downsizing and moves

**CHARLOTTE, N.C. — [Date]** — VaultXL, a company that manages belongings
during major life transitions, opened for business in [service area] today. The
company inventories, values and sells the contents of a home for families
settling an estate, downsizing, relocating or dividing a household, routing
items to the sale channel that pays the most for each category rather than
selling a house's contents to a single buyer at a single price.

[Paragraph 2 — the news itself: what launched, what it costs, who it serves,
what makes the method different. Concrete. This is the paragraph the reporter
will actually use.]

Founder Charles Dewhurst started the company after settling his own parents'
estates. His father died in January 2021; his mother died five months later. As
an only child with a newborn at home, he put both households into a storage unit
rather than face the work — despite having sold more than $1 million of
merchandise online over the prior decade.

> "I could price a pair of sneakers within five dollars from a photo, and I
> couldn't tell you what my mother's living room was worth," Dewhurst said.
> "Expertise wasn't what I was missing. Capacity was. Grief takes the exact part
> of your brain that settling an estate requires."
