# VaultXL founder story — how to use it

## Recommendation, short version

**Not a press release. A signed founder's letter on the site, plus four short
derivatives that all link back to it.**

A press release needs news, and a story is not news. It also puts your parents'
deaths into third-person corporate voice ("Dewhurst, who lost both parents in
2021, said...") which is the one register this material cannot survive. Keep the
release for the day there's an actual announcement — template is in
`press-release.md`.

## The files

| File | What it is | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| `our-story.md` | The canonical piece, ~950 words, first person, signed | `vaultxl.com/our-story` — one place only |
| `derivatives.md` | Homepage block, short bio, LinkedIn post, media pitch, first-client letter | Homepage, About, LinkedIn, inbox |
| `press-release.md` | Template + the criteria for when to send it | Hold |

Order of operations: publish `/our-story` first, then the homepage block that
links to it, then the LinkedIn post, then pitch media. The LinkedIn post is the
highest-value single use — estate attorneys, CPAs, realtors and senior-move
managers are your referral channel and they all live there.

## Fill these in before publishing

The drafts have bracketed gaps because I won't invent numbers for a VaultXL page:

- `[$XXX]/month` and `[N] years` on the storage unit — the single most persuasive
  number in the whole piece. It makes the thesis concrete.
- `[N] families` served so far. If it's two, say two. Small and true beats vague.
- Pricing model: commission range, minimums, typical walkthrough-to-settlement
  timeline. Google rewards transparent pricing and this audience needs it.
- Service area — write only what you actually serve today.
- The channel list in "The idea" — name the real ones you use.
- Your mother's cancer: the draft says breast cancer that moved into her bones,
  which is what I understood from your telling. Correct it if I have it wrong.

## Three judgment calls I made, all reversible

1. **I left out the January 6 / Capitol parallel.** It's the most memorable line
   you told me and I understand why it's welded to that date for you. But on a
   page whose readers are grieving families of every political stripe, it pulls
   the reader out of your father's hospital room and into an argument. Keep it
   for a longer interview or a personal essay, where you have room to hold it.

2. **I cut the basketball rankings claim.** "Ranked ahead of Stephen Curry by the
   recruiting services" is checkable, and someone will check it. The version that
   survives scrutiny is the flat fact: Division I offers, signed with Charlotte,
   played professionally in Israel — which your Eurobasket profile proves. Also,
   softened your line about the Lutz-era 49ers going to the tournament "eight out
   of ten years"; the real number is about five NCAA appearances across his
   tenure, still a strong program, and the true version can't be picked apart.

3. **I cut basketball from the VaultXL story almost entirely.** It's a great
   story and it belongs on your personal site and in your bio — it's just not
   load-bearing here. The through-line that makes VaultXL make sense is:
   *sneakers on eBay with my dad in 1999 → a decade of pricing other people's
   things → the one estate I couldn't price was my own parents'.* Basketball
   interrupts that line. It's in the short bio in `derivatives.md`.

## What I did not touch

Your father's work is public and verifiable — *Miami Herald*, *Dear America:
Letters Home from Vietnam*, *The Ancient Giants Who Ruled America* — so it stays
in, named. It's also the best answer to "why should I trust you with my
parents' things": you kept your own father's.

The line I'd protect above all others in edits, because it's the one that turns a
memoir into a business: *"Expertise wasn't what I was missing. Capacity was."*
