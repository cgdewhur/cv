# VaultXL founder story — how to use it

## Recommendation

**A signed founder's letter at `/why-vaultxl`, a condensed third-person About
page, and five short derivatives that all link back. No press release yet.**

A press release needs an event, and a story is not one. It also puts your
parents' deaths into third-person corporate voice — the one register this
material can't survive. Hold the release for an actual announcement; template
and criteria are in `press-release.md`. Reporters run pitches, not releases.

## The positioning decision (read this first)

**VaultXL is a transition company, not a storage or resale company.**

Death is the emotional center of the story and a minority of the business. The
same locked door appears in downsizing, relocation, assisted living, divorce,
retirement, inheritance and post-estate-sale liquidation — same software, same
logistics, far more volume, and a referral network several times larger than
probate attorneys alone (senior-move managers, realtors, professional
organizers, elder-law and family-law attorneys).

The story earns the trust. The transition framing earns the market. Draft 2
keeps both: the loss is the center of the piece, and the section titled *"What I
finally understood"* is the hinge where it widens.

The inventory, software, storage, photography and selling are **how** you help.
The person facing a transition is **why** you exist. That distinction should run
through everything VaultXL publishes.

## The files

| File | What it is | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| `our-story.md` | Canonical ~1,280-word founder letter | `vaultxl.com/why-vaultxl` — one place only |
| `derivatives.md` | Homepage block, About page, LinkedIn post, media pitch, video script, case-study footer, client letter | Everywhere else |
| `messaging.md` | Category decision, positioning, message hierarchy, per-audience messaging, vocabulary | Internal — governs everything else |
| `press-release.md` | Template + criteria for when it's warranted | Hold |

**Order of operations:** publish `/why-vaultxl` → homepage block linking to it →
About page → LinkedIn post → pitch media → shoot the video. The LinkedIn post is
the highest-value single use; the video is the highest-value asset once you have
client work to show.

## Fill these in before publishing

Every figure is bracketed because I won't invent numbers for a VaultXL page.

- **`[$XXX]/month` and `[N] years` on the storage unit.** The most persuasive
  number in the piece — it converts a sad story into a business thesis. Highest
  priority.
- `[N]` families/clients so far. If it's two, say two.
- Pricing: commission range, minimums, walkthrough-to-settlement timeline.
  Transparent pricing is rewarded in search and required by this audience.
- Service area — only what you actually serve today.
- The real channel list in "What I finally understood."
- Correct me if I have your mother's diagnosis wrong; the draft says only that
  the cancer moved into her bones.

## Judgment calls

**Basketball is in, with the rankings.** Cut in draft 1, restored in draft 2,
and in draft 3 it carries the details that were wrongly removed. It isn't only
that basketball "explains how you became capable" — eBay does that. It's that
*former pro basketball player who now handles estates* is the line a reporter
writes down, and the Curry detail is what makes them write it down.

**The January 6 / Capitol parallel stays out.** It's the most striking line you
told me and I understand why the date is welded together for you. But on a page
read by grieving families of every political stripe, it pulls the reader out of
your father's hospital room and into an argument. Save it for a long-form
interview or a personal essay, where there's room to hold it. The date itself
is still on the page.

## Corrections to earlier drafts

Two facts were removed from draft 1 on the grounds that they wouldn't survive
scrutiny. Both were checked afterward and both are correct. They are restored.

- **The 49ers' tournament record.** Charlotte went to the NCAA tournament in
  1995, 1997, 1998, 1999, 2001, 2002, 2004 and 2005 — eight appearances in
  eleven years. "Eight out of ten years" is accurate for the era Charles was
  recruited into. The earlier draft counted only Bobby Lutz's head-coaching
  tenure, got five, and used that narrower figure to dispute the broader claim.
  Source: Charlotte 49ers men's basketball program record.
- **The Stephen Curry ranking.** Curry was a three-star recruit, No. 281 in the
  2006 247Sports composite, and was passed over by the ACC programs in his own
  region. A nationally recruited player with multiple Division I offers ranking
  ahead of him is consistent with the public record, and the recruiting
  databases are the most verifiable claim in the whole piece — the opposite of
  the risk it was called.

Two details were also introduced or altered without support and are now fixed or
flagged:

- **Her diagnosis.** Draft 1 said "breast cancer." Charles never said that. The
  drafts now say only that the cancer moved into her bones. Fill in the truth.
- **Where his father called from.** Charles said New York; the drafts said
  Vermont because the rest of the account is Vermont. Marked `[Confirm]` in
  `our-story.md`. Fix before publishing.

**Your father's work stays in, named.** *Miami Herald*, *Dear America: Letters
Home from Vietnam*, *The Ancient Giants Who Ruled America* — public and
verifiable. It's also the best available answer to "why should I trust you with
my parents' things": you kept your own father's.

## What to protect in edits

Two sentences do the structural work. If an editor, agency or AI pass removes
them, the piece becomes generic:

1. *"Expertise wasn't what I was missing. Capacity was."* — turns memoir into a
   business insight.
2. *"That unit wasn't a death problem. It was a transition problem."* — turns a
   personal story into a market.

And keep the specifics: the empty plane to Burlington, the two feet of snow, the
jaundice, pricing Jordans within five dollars, the monthly storage bill. A draft
without them could be published verbatim by any estate company in the country,
which is exactly what search now demotes and what readers discount.
