# Service pages

Split by **trigger event**, not by task. Somebody searches "what to do with
parents house after death," not "estate inventory services."

Only one page is drafted here. The other five are briefs, because per the
Dewhurst content rules a service page needs a real example, real pricing, and
real timelines — and drafting them without those produces commodity pages that
get shipped anyway. Answer the brief, then the page writes itself.

## Shared template

1. **H1 = the trigger event in the customer's words**
2. The problem, stated specifically (not a value prop)
3. What we do — numbered, with what you receive at each step
4. One real example with numbers
5. Pricing range and timeline, stated plainly
6. FAQ — answers that differ per page, only questions really asked
7. Quiet CTA

## You're the executor. The house is still full.

Everything in it has to be identified, valued, divided or sold, and most of it
has to happen before the house can be listed. Meanwhile the people who could
have told you what any of it was are the reason you're doing this.

Most executors do one of two things. They call an estate-sale company, which
clears the house in a weekend at whatever walks through the door. Or they rent a
storage unit, which doesn't solve anything — it converts the problem into a
monthly bill while paper, textiles and finishes sit through a year of climate
swings.

There's a third option, and it's the one we built.

## How it works

1. **Walkthrough.** We come to the house. No charge, no obligation, and we tell
   you honestly if the contents don't justify our involvement.
2. **Inventory.** Everything photographed and catalogued, room by room. You get
   the file whether or not you hire us for the sale.
3. **Research and valuation.** Each category priced against what it has actually
   sold for — comparable sales, not a guess and not a flat per-room number.
   Confident numbers are marked as such; ranges mean we want a second opinion.
4. **The plan.** Item by item: sell, and where; keep; donate with the receipt
   for the estate; or dispose. You approve it before anything moves.
5. **Execution.** Each piece goes to the channel that pays the most for it —
   [auction / marketplace / dealer network / direct buyer] — instead of one
   buyer taking the house at one price.
6. **Settlement.** Itemized accounting of what sold, where, and for how much.
   Documentation your attorney can put in the file.

Nothing is sold without your written sign-off. Anything you flag as staying with
the family stays — no questions, no pressure — including the most valuable thing
in the room.

# BRIEFS — answer these, then draft

Each needs the same five things: a real number, a real place, a real item, a real
decision with its tradeoff, and pricing.

### `/downsizing` — retiree leaving a long-held house
Register: capable adult making a choice, not decline. H1 candidate: *"Forty
years fits in a four-bedroom. It doesn't fit in the condo."*
**Need:** a real downsize — neighborhood, years in the house, what came along,
what it returned, how long it took. Pricing for a live-in client (different from
an estate: they're present, they change their minds, that costs time).

### `/divorce` — household division
Register: neutral, procedural, zero sentiment. The product is a defensible
inventory both attorneys accept.
**Need:** do you take instruction from one party or both? Who signs? How is a
disputed item handled? Have you done one yet — if not, say so and price it as
new work rather than implying experience.

### `/relocation` — job move on a deadline
Register: logistics. The pitch is weight: movers price by it, so selling before
the truck comes is money twice.
**Need:** a real before/after — moving quote at full weight vs. after, plus what
the sold items returned. That single comparison is the whole page.

### `/assisted-living` — adult child moving a parent
Register: gentlest of the six. The buyer is the child; the subject is the
parent, who is alive and should be treated as present.
**Need:** how do you involve the parent? What's the policy on items they want to
keep but can't fit? Local facility relationships, if any.

### `/business-liquidation` — closure, downsizing, equipment
Register: purely commercial, no founder story.
**Need:** asset classes you'll actually take. Recovery rate vs. auctioneer
hammer price on a real job. Whether you handle removal.

### `/partners` — attorneys, realtors, move managers, organizers
Not a service page. Lead with documentation and reliability; the story is the
second paragraph and it works there — you've been the overwhelmed client they
handle every week.
**Need:** what you commit to on their timeline, what they receive for the file,
and whether there's a referral arrangement. Say so if there isn't.
